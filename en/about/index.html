About

Arnaud de Turckheim

An engineer by training, a photographer since the 2000s. A practice kept private for nearly twenty years, made public in 2026.

Born in Paris in 1990, Arnaud de Turckheim grew up between Paris and Provence, in a family where the image is a familiar language: his mother is a photographer. An engineer by training, he lived in China, England and then Japan before returning to France in 2017.

He has been taking photographs since the 2000s. A practice long kept to himself, nearly twenty years of images held back, which he makes public for the first time in November 2026 with Ce Qui Demeure, curated by Pierre El Khoury.

From his nomadic years he has kept a taste for discreet observation. Friends readily describe him as a “geek cowboy”, torn between the engineer’s logic and the pull of wide open spaces. His style is recognisable: sustained backlight, raking light, frank contrasts, shades of grey and green.

One word often comes up about his work: intense. It speaks to the way he stands before his subjects, both inside and outside the moment, in that attentive in-between that makes photography.

Statement

What Surfaces in the Ordinary

Arnaud de Turckheim photographs what offers itself without being asked for. His images are born neither of a commission nor of staging: two workers and their shadows on a platform in Shimoda, a level crossing in Nakano, an empty swimming pool in Los Angeles, a baobab in the Sine-Saloum. Nothing spectacular, only the precise moment when the ordinary tips over.

His eye refuses the picturesque. No folkloric Japan, no postcard Africa, no movie-set America: he looks for the blind spot of the cliché. Often a single figure holds a wide frame. Light, raking or backlit, sculpts as much as it reveals. The images breathe rather than speak.

These are digital photographs, honest ones, framed by the eye and not by a model. Made before the generative shift, they belong to a time when an image was found rather than generated.
